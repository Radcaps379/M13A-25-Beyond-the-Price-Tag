# Recruitment Committee Brief — Diego Coppola

**Defender · Hellas Verona · Italy – Serie A · age 21.0**

## Recommendation

Diego Coppola is recommended for further investigation, not for an approach. He carries the largest valuation gap in the recommended portfolio, and that fact warrants caution rather than enthusiasm: the analytical system has established that large unexplained gaps are associated with elevated exit risk rather than with subsequent returns.

## Why the player merits consideration

Coppola sits in the 89th percentile for performance among defenders. His development potential score is 0.691 and his value efficiency score is 0.931, the highest in the portfolio. He played 2,926 league minutes over the season, the largest workload of the three recommended players.

## Performance and valuation evidence

Current market value is EUR 10.0m. The model-implied value is EUR 27.1m, a difference of 171.4%.

The factors raising the model's valuation are age (+0.955), league minutes played (+0.336), height (+0.138), minutes per appearance (+0.114) and league context (+0.081).

The factors lowering it are goal contributions per 90 (-0.179), playing in Italy – Serie A (-0.155), club squad size and rotation (-0.140), three-season average minutes (-0.053) and assists per 90 (-0.026).

One aspect of this decomposition warrants explanation. His goal contributions per 90 of 0.062 reduce the model's estimate. This is the intended behaviour for a defender: the model derives defensive value from availability and minutes rather than attacking output, and low attacking output is consistent with the role rather than evidence against the player.

## Why the optimizer selected him

He was selected under a EUR 50m budget with one defender, one midfielder and one attacker required. He scored on quality at the 89th percentile, development potential 0.69, value efficiency 0.93, against a risk component of 0.33.

## Key risks and uncertainties

His predicted probability of falling below 900 top-5 league minutes next season is 12.9%, the lowest in the portfolio. This estimate comes from a model with a held-out AUC of 0.732.

Model uncertainty for his segment scores 0.788, the highest of the three recommended players. The model's estimate for this player is therefore less reliable than for the others, and the valuation model carries a median error of 43.9% on held-out data.

Two limitations compound here. The 171.4% gap is the largest in the portfolio, and the model systematically under-values players aged 21 and under — a pattern that replicates across three consecutive seasons. Part of the apparent gap may therefore reflect known model bias rather than a market error.

## What the model does not know

The system observes match statistics, age, position, league and valuation history. It has no information on contract length or expiry, injury history or current fitness, scouting or tactical assessment, off-field circumstances, or agent and release-clause situation.

The back-test finding applies with particular force to this player: the hypothesis that model residuals identify exploitable undervaluation was tested out-of-sample and was not supported (p = 0.307), and players flagged by residual left top-5 football at 3.7x the benchmark rate (30.1% versus 8.2%, p = 2.6e-11). The size of the gap here is a reason for closer scrutiny, not for confidence.

## Recommended human due diligence

1. Verify contract length, expiry and any release clause.
2. Obtain injury and fitness history from medical staff, given his high minutes load.
3. Commission live scouting on defensive quality, which the model measures only indirectly through availability.
4. Establish the selling club's position and the player's willingness to move.
5. Investigate specifically why the market values him at EUR 10.0m when his minutes and age profile imply more. The reason is not observable in the supplied data, and identifying it should precede any further consideration.
