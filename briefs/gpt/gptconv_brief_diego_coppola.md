# Recruitment Committee Brief — Diego Coppola

**Defender · Hellas Verona · Italy – Serie A · age 21.0**

## Recommendation

Diego Coppola is recommended for further investigation, not for an immediate approach. He has the largest valuation gap in the illustrative portfolio, but the project found that valuation residuals did not identify systematically exploitable appreciation out of sample. His gap should therefore be treated as a reason to investigate what the public model cannot observe.

## Why the player merits consideration

Coppola ranks at the 89th percentile for quality. His development potential score is 0.691 and his value-efficiency score is 0.931, the highest of the three selected players. He played 2,926 league minutes during the season, giving the model a substantial performance sample.

## Performance and valuation evidence

His current market value is EUR 10.0m, compared with a model-implied value of EUR 27.1m, a valuation gap of 171.4%.

The strongest positive SHAP drivers are age (+0.955), league minutes played (+0.336), height (+0.138), minutes per appearance (+0.114) and the encoded league-context contribution (+0.081).

The main negative drivers are goal contributions per 90 (-0.179), playing in Italy – Serie A (-0.155), club squad size / rotation (-0.140), three-season average minutes (-0.053) and assists per 90 (-0.026).

The negative contribution from goal contributions per 90 is consistent with the model's position-sensitive structure: Coppola is evaluated as a defender, so attacking output is not treated as the principal source of defensive value.

## Why the optimizer selected him

Coppola was selected under the EUR 50m scenario requiring one defender, one midfielder and one attacker. He combines 89th-percentile quality with development potential of 0.69 and value efficiency of 0.93, against a risk component of 0.33.

## Key risks and uncertainties

His predicted probability of falling below 900 top-5 league minutes in the following season is 12.9%. The exit-risk model has a held-out AUC of 0.732.

His model-uncertainty score is 0.788, the highest among the three selected players. The valuation model also has a median held-out percentage error of 43.9%.

The 171.4% valuation gap is therefore not a reason for confidence by itself. The project found that residual-based valuation discrepancies were not supported as an exploitable appreciation signal.

## What the model does not know

The system has no information on contract length and expiry, injury history or current fitness, scouting and tactical assessment, off-field circumstances or player willingness, or agent and release-clause situation.

The project found that players flagged by the valuation residual left top-five football at 3.7x the benchmark rate (30.1% versus 8.2%, p = 2.6e-11). This is a population-level relationship, not a prediction that Coppola will leave.

The size of his model-market discrepancy should therefore prompt investigation of the information held by the market but absent from the public dataset.

## Recommended human due diligence

1. Verify contract length, expiry and any release clause.
2. Obtain medical and fitness information from the appropriate specialists.
3. Conduct detailed defensive scouting and assess tactical fit.
4. Establish the selling club's position and the player's willingness to move.
5. Identify why the market value is EUR 10.0m despite the model-implied value of EUR 27.1m before treating the discrepancy as an opportunity.
