# Recruitment Committee Brief — Facundo Buonanotte

**Midfield · Leicester City · England – Premier League · age 20.0**

## Recommendation

Facundo Buonanotte is recommended for further investigation within the illustrative portfolio, not because he presents a large valuation discrepancy, but because his quality and development profile fit the portfolio requirements. His market value of EUR 20.0m is close to the model-implied value of EUR 20.3m, so this is explicitly not a bargain-based recommendation.

## Why the player merits consideration

Buonanotte ranks at the 86th percentile for quality. His development potential score is 0.767 and his value-efficiency score is 0.567. He played 1,514 league minutes during the season.

His principal case for inclusion is therefore the combination of quality, age and development potential rather than a large model-market gap.

## Performance and valuation evidence

His current market value is EUR 20.0m and the model-implied value is EUR 20.3m, corresponding to a valuation gap of only 1.5%.

The strongest positive SHAP drivers are age (+0.764), goal contributions per 90 (+0.137), and the combined league-context contribution associated with his Premier League setting (+0.344).

The main negative drivers are league minutes played (-0.143), minutes per appearance (-0.099), club squad size / rotation (-0.082), disciplinary record (-0.055) and three-season average minutes (-0.051).

The SHAP values explain the model's valuation. They do not establish that any individual market price is correct or incorrect.

## Why the optimizer selected him

Buonanotte was selected under the EUR 50m scenario requiring one defender, one midfielder and one attacker. He combines 86th-percentile quality with development potential of 0.77 and value efficiency of 0.57, against a risk component of 0.24.

The important distinction is that the optimizer did not need a large valuation gap to select him. His inclusion demonstrates that the final decision framework considers quality and development potential alongside affordability and risk.

## Key risks and uncertainties

His predicted probability of falling below 900 top-5 league minutes in the following season is 24.9%. The exit-risk model has a held-out AUC of 0.732.

His model-uncertainty score is 0.219, below Coppola's but above Stassin's. His 1.5% valuation gap provides little evidence of a market discrepancy, so the selection rests primarily on non-price characteristics.

## What the model does not know

The system has no information on contract length and expiry, injury history or current fitness, scouting and tactical assessment, off-field circumstances or player willingness, or agent and release-clause situation.

The project's back-test did not support interpreting valuation residuals as systematically exploitable undervaluation. The system should therefore use the valuation gap as contextual evidence rather than as a standalone buying signal.

## Recommended human due diligence

1. Verify contract length, expiry and any release clause.
2. Review injury and fitness information outside the public dataset.
3. Conduct detailed scouting of midfield role, tactical fit and expected usage.
4. Establish the player's willingness to move and the selling club's position.
5. Assess whether the quality and development profile justifies the EUR 20.0m acquisition benchmark independently of any small valuation discrepancy.
